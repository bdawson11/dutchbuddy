# GermanBuddy — 84-Day Curriculum Roadmap (`german-de`)

*The per-language worked example for German, built against the unchanged LanguageBuddy
engine. Mirrors the structure of `plan.md` §3 but is **designed from German's own
grammar spine**, not translated from Dutch. This doc is the locked scope input for the
weekly lesson-generation batches (see `plan.md` §4).*

---

## 1. Dialect identity

- **Identity:** Germany Standard German — *Hochdeutsch*, northern-neutral (the register of national TV news, Berlin/Hamburg everyday speech, and standard textbooks).
- **Contrast — this is NOT:**
  - **Austrian German** — no *Grüß Gott / Servus* as default, no *Jänner* (→ Januar), *Sackerl* (→ Tüte), *Semmel* (→ Brötchen), *Erdapfel* (→ Kartoffel), no *heuer*.
  - **Swiss German** — no *Grüezi*, and **ß is kept** (Switzerland writes *ss* everywhere).
  - Regional German is acknowledged and enjoyed as *color* (Cologne Rhineland pride, Bavaria at the finale), but never taught as the target norm.
- **Northern-neutral markers to lean on:** *doch, mal, halt, genau, na ja, tja, Quatsch, alles klar, Moin* (northern hello), *Feierabend* (the end-of-work-day concept with no English equivalent), *passt, Bock haben*.
- **Register anchor:** we teach the German a motivated adult actually hears standing in a Berlin *Bäckerei* queue — not stiff *Amtsdeutsch*, not slang-first street German.

---

## 2. The grammar spine

Four structural hard parts anchor the entire sequence. Everything is ordered so each spine is introduced at the moment the learner needs it and then revisited relentlessly.

- **① The case system, sequenced nom → acc → dat (→ light gen).** German's defining difficulty. We do **not** dump four cases at once. **Nominative** is assumed from W1 (subjects, *sein*-predicates). **Accusative** opens W3 (family: *ich habe einen Bruder*; acc prepositions *für/ohne/gegen/um/durch* as a clean anchor). **Dative** opens W6 (health: *mir tut der Kopf weh*) and is mastered in W10 (dative prepositions + verbs). **Genitive** gets a light B1 touch only (W9). Case endings on *der/die/das*, *ein/kein/mein*, and pronouns are drilled at each stage — **but attributive adjective endings are deliberately deferred** (see ④).
- **② V2 word order + verb-final subclauses.** The finite verb is **second** in main clauses (introduced W2 as law, revisited constantly); in subordinate clauses (*weil, dass, wenn, obwohl…*) it goes to the **end**. The subclause flip is the explicit **A2→B1 gate** (W8, deepened W9).
- **③ Separable verbs + the Perfekt as the spoken past.** German's everyday past is the **Perfekt** (*ich habe gemacht / ich bin gefahren*), taught mid-course (W5) — earlier than a Romance-language preterite. **Separable verbs** (*aufstehen → ich bin aufgestanden*, the *ge-* landing inside) are woven in immediately (W5) because they interact with both Perfekt and V2.
- **④ Adjective endings as the B1 polish.** Attributive endings (*ein guter Kaffee / den guten Kaffee / mit dem guten Kaffee*) are the single most ending-heavy corner of German because they encode gender **and** case **and** article-type at once. We keep A1/A2 on **predicate** adjectives only (*der Kaffee ist gut* — no ending) so nobody drowns, and treat the full attributive-ending system as the **B1 finishing move** (introduced in the M11 "sounding German" arc, applied through the finale). This is a deliberate pedagogy call: get the learner *talking* with correct cases first; make endings the polish, not the gate.

*Why this order beats a Dutch translation:* Dutch's spine is V2 + perfect-as-past + verb-final subclauses + *er*. German shares V2, perfect-as-past, and verb-final subclauses — but its true center of gravity is the **case system**, which Dutch has almost entirely lost. So German's sequence is rebuilt around cases (nom→acc→dat→endings) with the shared spines slotted in where the cases make room for them.

## 3. Weekly rhythm

Mirrors the engine's fixed rhythm:
- **Weeks 1–2:** 6 teaching days + 1 capstone.
- **Weeks 3–12:** 5 teaching days + 1 review/immersion day + 1 capstone.
- Every **capstone** ends with the growing self-intro ("*Meine Geschichte*", §8).
- Every **review/immersion** day carries the recurring-cast story arc (§6).

## 4. The 84 days

### A1 Foundations — Weeks 1–4
*Greet, introduce yourself, order at the Bäckerei, describe your day.*

**Week 1 — German sounds & first words · M01**
1. Laute I — the umlauts *ä/ö/ü*, long vs short vowels, and the meaning they carry (*Mutter* vs *Mütter*, *schon* vs *schön*)
2. Laute II — *ch* (ich-Laut *ich* vs ach-Laut *Bach*), *ß* vs *ss*, uvular *r*, the *s/z/w/v/j* traps; first tongue-twisters
3. Begrüßungen & die Bäckerei — Hallo/Guten Tag/Moin/Tschüss, first Bäckerei scene, *bitte/danke*
4. Pronomen + *sein* — ich/du/Sie/er/sie/es/wir/ihr/sie; the **du vs Sie** register intro
5. *haben* + negation — *kein* vs *nicht* (the placement rule)
6. der/die/das — noun gender first contact, plural overview, why gender rules everything later (it drives the cases)
7. **Capstone** — first real self-intro + journal (present, *sein/haben*)

**Week 2 — Numbers, time & the V2 engine · M02**
8. Zahlen 0–100 — incl. the reversed units (*einundzwanzig* = one-and-twenty), one word
9. Big numbers, prices, euro amounts, phone numbers
10. Uhrzeit, Tage, Monate — *halb sechs* = 5:30 (the classic trap), *Viertel vor/nach*
11. Präsens + **V2 word order** — regular conjugation, verb-second as law
12. Fragen & Inversion — yes/no inversion, W-Fragen (*wer/was/wo/wann/wie/warum*)
13. *es gibt* + dieser/diese/dieses — pointing at the world (first live accusative via *es gibt*)
14. **Capstone** — daily-routine production day

**Week 3 — People, places & the accusative · M03**  *(case spine ① opens: nom → acc)*
15. Familie + **Akkusativ I** — *der → den* (*ich habe einen Bruder, eine Schwester, ein Kind*)
16. Beschreiben — **predicate** adjectives only (*er ist nett, groß, lustig*) — no endings yet (that's the B1 move)
17. Possessivartikel (*mein/dein/sein/ihr*) in the accusative (*meinen Bruder*); showing photos
18. Dein Kiez — neighborhood vocab, Berlin *Kiez* culture, address & city talk
19. **Akkusativ-Präpositionen** — *für, ohne, gegen, um, durch* (always + acc): a clean anchor to lock the case in
20. **Review + immersion** — meet **Lena** narrating her Berlin *Kiez* (accusative in the wild)
21. **Capstone** — family + Kiez portrait

**Week 4 — Food, the Bäckerei & the modal system · M04**
22. Essen — Brot/Brötchen culture, meal structure (*Frühstück/Mittagessen/Abendbrot*), German meal times
23. Bestellen in Bäckerei & Café — *ich hätte gern, ich nehme, für mich, bitte*
24. **Modalverben** — *können/wollen/müssen/dürfen/möchten* (the politeness engine; infinitive to the end)
25. Mögen & *gern* — likes: *ich mag…, ich esse gern…*; the *gern* trick as universal approval
26. Supermarkt & **Pfand** — Rewe/Aldi, the deposit-bottle system, quantities (*ein Kilo, 200 Gramm*), paying, the bag-it-fast checkout
27. **Review + immersion** — **Klaus**'s routine and his favorite Bäckerei/Imbiss in Cologne
28. **Capstone** — full-A1 production: order, describe, introduce + food/drink + a modal wish

### A2 Building — Weeks 5–8
*Tell stories in the past. Travel, health, everyday life.*

**Week 5 — The Perfekt (German's everyday past) · M05**  *(spine ③)*
29. Perfekt with *haben* — *ge-…-t* participles (*gemacht, gesagt, gekauft*)
30. Perfekt with *sein* — motion/change verbs (*gegangen, gefahren, geblieben*); the haben/sein choice
31. Irregular participles — the top 15 (*gewesen, gegangen, gesehen, gegessen, getrunken, gekommen, gefahren…*)
32. **Separable verbs** — *aufstehen, mitnehmen, ankommen* in present and Perfekt (*aufgestanden* — the *ge-* lands inside)
33. Reisen & die Bahn — Deutsche Bahn, S-Bahn/U-Bahn, *umsteigen*, *Verspätung*, platform announcements
34. **Review + immersion** — Lena's weekend trip to the Ostsee (Perfekt narrative)
35. **Capstone** — "Was hast du gemacht?" storytelling with Jonas

**Week 6 — Health, feelings, advice & the dative · M06**  *(case spine ① continues: dat opens)*
36. Der Körper — body vocab, pain patterns: *ich habe Kopfschmerzen* AND *mir tut der Kopf weh* (first live dative)
37. Beim Arzt — the German Hausarzt/Krankenkasse system, *Termin*, *Krankschreibung* dialogue
38. Notfälle — key phrases, *Hilfe / ruf 112*, the Apotheke scene (rezeptfrei culture)
39. Gefühle — *froh/wütend/müde/nervös*; *sich fühlen* intro
40. Ratschläge geben — *du musst, du solltest, an deiner Stelle würde ich…* (preview of *würde*); dative *mir/dir*
41. **Review + immersion** — **Aylin**'s rough week (bike stolen, gets sick, goes to the Hausarzt)
42. **Capstone** — "Was ist passiert?" production day + feelings/health self-intro layer

**Week 7 — The other past: Präteritum & memories · M07**
43. Präteritum — regular *-te* (*machte, sagte*) + the spoken-in-speech set *war/hatte/konnte/wollte*
44. Perfekt vs Präteritum — the usage split (spoken Perfekt vs narrative/written Präteritum; *sein/haben*/modals in speech)
45. *Als ich klein war* — childhood vocab, school memories, strong Präteritum (*ging, kam, sah, gab*)
46. Describing places & weather in the past (*es war, es gab, es regnete*)
47. Früher vs heute — habitual past, frequency adverbs (*immer … nie*)
48. **Review + immersion** — Klaus's childhood in Cologne (Präteritum narrative, Karneval)
49. **Capstone** — childhood portrait woven into the growing self-intro

**Week 8 — Future, opinions & the subclause gate · M08**  *(spine ②)*
50. Futur — *werden* + infinitive **and** present-for-future (*nächste Woche fahre ich…*); time markers
51. *wollen/möchten* for plans; *Sollen wir…?* for offers & suggestions
52. Meinungen — *ich finde, ich glaube, meiner Meinung nach* + agreement ladder
53. **Subordinate clauses I** — *weil, dass, wenn* and **verb-final order** (the A2→B1 gate module)
54. Komparativ & Superlativ — *größer als, am größten*, irregulars (*gut/besser/am besten*)
55. **Review + immersion** — Lena & Jonas debate: must expats learn German?
56. **Capstone** — "Mein kommendes Jahr" production day (plan + opinion + subclause)

### B1 Threshold — Weeks 9–12
*Opinions, hypotheticals, real conversations — and holding your ground in German.*

**Week 9 — Complex sentences · M09**
57. Subordinate clauses II — full conjunction set (*obwohl, während, damit, bevor, nachdem, seit*); genitive light touch (*wegen*)
58. Relative clauses — *der/die/das* as relative pronouns incl. dative *dem/denen*; case decided *inside* the clause
59. Indirect speech — *er hat gesagt, dass…*; reporting questions with *ob*
60. Konjunktiv II with *würde* — polite requests, hypotheticals, advice (*ich würde…, ich könnte…, ich hätte gern*)
61. *wenn*-clauses — real and hypothetical conditions (*Wenn ich Zeit hätte…*)
62. **Review + immersion** — **Aylin's news: a job offer in Munich**, told via reported speech (the reported-speech arc)
63. **Capstone** — "die Neuigkeiten" gossip production with Jonas (reported-speech layer)

**Week 10 — Dative mastery, two-way prepositions & verb clusters · M10**  *(the dense module — German's fear-factor week)*
64. **Dativ II** — dative prepositions (*mit, nach, bei, seit, von, zu, aus*) + dative verbs (*helfen, danken, gefallen*)
65. **Wechselpräpositionen** — the two-way prepositions (*in/auf/an/unter/über/vor/hinter/neben/zwischen*): **Akkusativ for motion (wohin)** vs **Dativ for location (wo)** — the module every German learner fears; given a full deliberate day
66. Passiv with *werden* — signs and news language (*hier wird gearbeitet*)
67. Reflexive verbs — *sich fühlen, sich freuen, sich vorstellen*; *um … zu* constructions
68. Two-verb & verb-cluster word order — *ich will morgen nach Hause fahren*; final-cluster order in subclauses
69. **Review + immersion** — **Aylin takes the job and moves Berlin → Munich.** Moving-day (*Umzug*) conversation braiding all M10 grammar (dative + two-way prepositions). **THE mid-arc life event.**
70. **Capstone** — "meine große Entscheidung" letter + portrait (decision + subclause + dative layer)

**Week 11 — Sounding German · M11**  *(adjective endings ④ land here as the polish)*
71. Verb-preposition pairs — *warten auf, denken an, sich freuen auf/über* (15 core pairs, each with its fixed case)
72. **Modalpartikeln** — *doch, mal, halt, ja, eben, wohl, schon* — the soul of spoken German; blunt-vs-soft contrast pairs
73. **Adjektivendungen** + diminutives — the attributive-ending system (*ein guter Kaffee / den guten Kaffee / mit dem guten Kaffee*) introduced as B1 polish, plus *-chen/-lein* (*Brötchen, Häuschen*) and where Bavarian *-erl* diverges from Hochdeutsch *-chen*
74. **META-MODULE — *Fälle in Echtzeit*** (cases in real time): surviving the case system while actually talking. How not to freeze over *der/dem/den*, default-and-repair strategies, that natives forgive slips, confidence scripts. *German's language-specific meta-module slot (Dutch's is "blijf in het Nederlands"; German's is the case system under time pressure).*
75. **du vs Sie mastery** — *Siezen* vs *Duzen* calibration by context: *das Du anbieten*, regional & generational differences, how to recover a mis-call
76. **Review + immersion** — Lena visits Aylin in Munich (Saturday catch-up braiding M11; a light Bavarian-flavor cameo that reinforces we speak Hochdeutsch)
77. **Capstone** — "mein Wiedersehen" production day (register control: the same intro said *Sie* vs *du*)

**Week 12 — Street German & the finale · M12**
78. Imperative — commands (*Komm! Warte mal! Pass auf!*), softeners (*mal, doch, bitte*), the Sie-imperative (*Kommen Sie*)
79. **Slang traffic-light** — green (*super, cool, Quatsch, alles klar, Feierabend*), yellow (*geil, krass, Alter, Bock, ey*), red (harsher profanity — flagged, explained, use-with-care)
80. Kneipe & Feierabend culture — ordering flow (*ein Bier, eine Schorle, Pfand*), rounds, *Prost*, the bill (*getrennt oder zusammen?*)
81. Beim Friseur / im Geschäft — service scenes, small-talk scripts
82. Redewendungen — 12 idioms that don't translate (*Ich verstehe nur Bahnhof; Das ist mir Wurst; Tomaten auf den Augen haben…*)
83. **Final immersion** — Lena, Jonas & Aylin at a Kneipe, one year on: every M12 move braided
84. **Final capstone — *Meine Geschichte*** — 12-week recap, letter to your past self (one tense-move per slot), definitive B1 self-intro, German farewells, celebratory stats banner

## 7. Register / slang traffic-light

Full authoring rules in `style-guide.md`; the tiers:

- 🟢 **Green — use freely:** *doch, mal, halt, genau, na ja, alles klar, Quatsch, super, cool, passt, Feierabend, Prost, Tschüss, Moin*. Neutral-to-friendly everywhere.
- 🟡 **Yellow — casual, mind the room:** *geil* (means "awesome" but literally "horny" — fine among friends, never with your boss or the *Amt*), *krass, Alter* (dude), *ey, Bock haben* (to be up for), *nervig, Schnauze* (shut it — rude), *Scheiße* (mild but public-audible). Friends only; never in formal/Sie contexts.
- 🔴 **Red — flagged, explained, use-with-care:** stronger profanity (*verpiss dich*, *Arschloch*) and slurs. German cursing skews **scatological and religious** (*Scheiße, verdammt, Herrgott*) rather than disease-based — an explicit contrast we teach so learners *recognize* these without deploying them. Presented for comprehension and boundary-awareness, never drilled for production.

## 8. The day-74 meta-module

**Slot:** day 74, "*Fälle in Echtzeit*" (Cases in Real Time). German's language-specific challenge module — the equivalent of Dutch's "blijf in het Nederlands" day, but pointed at German's own core pain: **producing correct cases at conversational speed.** Contents: why the freeze happens (four cases × three genders × article types), the default-and-repair move (say it, self-correct lightly, keep going), the reassurance that natives read *meaning* through wrong endings, and confidence scripts (*"Moment, wie heißt das noch… egal —"*). Register mastery (du/Sie) is handled separately the next day (75), mirroring Dutch's day-74/75 split.

## 9. Locale & audio config

- **`locale`: `de-DE`** — drives Web Speech API voice selection (v1) and any v2 pre-generated TTS.
- **Voice availability:** `de-DE` is one of the best-supported Web Speech locales (Google Deutsch, and OS voices like *Anna*/*Petra*/*Markus*). Northern-neutral output — avoid explicitly Austrian (`de-AT`) or Swiss (`de-CH`) voices if the platform exposes them.
- **Orthography:** content uses proper *ä/ö/ü* and *ß* (never the *ae/oe/ue/ss* fallbacks). Grading is diacritic-tolerant so a learner typing *ue* for *ü* or *ss* for *ß* still passes, with the correct form shown as the gentle correction.
- **`speak` text:** always the exact spoken German (umlauts and ß included); the engine hands it to `speak(text, {lang: 'de-DE'})`.

## 11. Season 2 — Weeks 13–18: B2 Depth → C1 Gateway

*Season 1 got the learner **talking** — cases held together, a real conversation survived, a B1 self-intro delivered. Season 2 is the leap from "can hold their ground" to "sounds educated": the moods (Konjunktiv II and I), the full passive, the attributive precision of written German, and the register agility a professional life in Germany demands. Same unchanged engine, same weekly rhythm (5 teaching + 1 review/immersion + 1 capstone), same growing self-intro. Designed from German's **upper-level** pain points, not translated from any other pack.*

**Honest framing (non-negotiable):** W13–16 are **B2** ("B2 Depth"). W17–18 are the **C1 Gateway** — they *prepare the leap* to C1; they do **not** certify C1. We say so plainly to the learner. No one finishes day 126 a C1 speaker; they finish it ready to train toward one, with the right reflexes started.

### German's upper-level pain points (the design inputs)

- **Konjunktiv II mastery** — *würde/hätte/wäre*, present *and* past hypotheticals (*wenn ich Zeit hätte… / wenn ich das gewusst hätte…*), and polite hedging (*ich hätte da eine Frage; könnten Sie…; ich würde sagen…*). B1 only previewed *würde*; B2 makes it reflex, including the past (*hätte gemacht, wäre gegangen*) and the strong one-word forms (*käme, ginge, wüsste, bräuchte*).
- **Konjunktiv I + reported speech (news German)** — *er sagte, er habe/sei/komme*; *die Sprecherin erklärte, man werde prüfen…*. A genuine C1 recognition marker: the mood of TV news, press, and formal reporting. Taught for **recognition first**, controlled production second.
- **The full passive system + Nominalstil** — Vorgangspassiv (*es wird gebaut*) vs Zustandspassiv (*es ist gebaut*), the agent split *von* (doer) vs *durch* (means), *werden*-passive across tenses, *sein + zu* + Infinitiv (*das ist noch zu klären*), and the **Nominalstil** of bureaucratic/professional German (*die Bearbeitung Ihres Antrags* rather than *wir bearbeiten…*). Amtsdeutsch survival: decode it, don't drown in it.
- **Participial attributes** — the compression that defines written German: *die im Bericht genannten Zahlen*, *ein gerade erst eröffnetes Café*, *das zu lösende Problem*. Partizip I/II (and *zu* + Partizip I) squeezed in front of the noun where English needs a relative clause.
- **Adjective-ending automaticity** — W11 introduced the attributive system; W16 **drills it to reflex** under time pressure, so the ending arrives without a table lookup. The B2 move: stop *computing* endings and start *producing* them.
- **Relative-clause depth + wo-/da- compounds** — relatives with prepositions and genitive (*dessen/deren*), *was/wo(-)* relatives (*das, was… / die Stadt, wo…*), and the *da-/wo-*compounds (*dafür, darüber, worauf, womit*) that replace prepositional pronouns for things.
- **Word formation** — the generative engine of German vocabulary: separable/inseparable prefixes for meaning (*ver-, zer-, ent-, be-, er-*), nominalizing suffixes (*-ung, -keit, -heit, -schaft, -nis*), *un-* negation, and reading/coining nominal compounds (*die Mitarbeiterzufriedenheitsbefragung*). Guess and build words instead of memorizing them.
- **Professional German** — the Bewerbung (Anschreiben + Lebenslauf conventions), the Vorstellungsgespräch, formal E-Mail etiquette (*Sehr geehrte… / Mit freundlichen Grüßen*, the du-Mail vs Sie-Mail divide), and Amtsdeutsch survival. The C1-gateway payoff: presenting yourself as a professional adult in German.
- **Fast colloquial speech + Modalpartikel mastery** — decoding real spoken tempo (*haste, kannste, gibt's, ne?, wa?*), reductions and swallowed syllables, and stacking Modalpartikeln (*das ist halt eben doch nicht so einfach*) the way natives actually do. Comprehension of speed, not just clarity.
- **Regional cameo (exposure only)** — a Bavarian/Austrian contrast (Aylin's Munich, Vroni's Vienna: *Servus, Grüß Gott, Jänner, Sackerl, heuer, Erdapfel*) enjoyed as **color**, sharpening the learner's ownership of Hochdeutsch as their norm. Never taught as target.

### The 42 days (85–126)

#### B2 Depth — Weeks 13–16

**Week 13 — Konjunktiv II: das Hypothetische · M13** *(level B2)*
85. *würde* + Infinitiv as the workhorse (*ich würde sagen…, an deiner Stelle würde ich…*) — recap and extend from B1
86. The strong one-word Konjunktiv II — *wäre, hätte, könnte, müsste, würde*, plus *käme, ginge, wüsste, bräuchte*; when the short form beats *würde*
87. Past hypotheticals — *hätte gemacht / wäre gegangen*; *Wenn ich das gewusst hätte, wäre ich nicht gekommen* (regret & the counterfactual)
88. Polite hedging in the wild — *ich hätte da eine Frage; könnten Sie mir…; es wäre schön, wenn…; ich würde ungern…* (softening as a social skill)
89. *als ob / als wenn* + Konjunktiv II — *er tut so, als ob er alles wüsste*; wishes (*wenn ich nur…!*), and *beinahe/fast* + past subjunctive
90. **Review + immersion** — Lena & Jonas play "*Was würdest du tun, wenn…*"; Jonas half-jokes about quitting the bookshop for music (the seed). Konjunktiv II in the wild.

**Week 14 — Konjunktiv I & indirekte Rede: Nachrichtendeutsch · M14** *(level B2)*
92. Konjunktiv I forms — *er habe, sei, komme, werde, könne*; the *sei*/plural *seien* backbone
93. Indirekte Rede — turning direct speech into reported (*"Ich komme" → er sagte, er komme*); tense backshift logic
94. When Konjunktiv I collapses into II — *sie sagten, sie hätten* (identical-to-present forms escape to Konjunktiv II); the native repair
95. News German — *die Sprecherin erklärte, man werde prüfen…*; reading a short Tagesschau-style report for the mood, not the vocab
96. Argumentation connectors — *einerseits… andererseits, zwar… aber, dennoch, trotzdem, folglich, hingegen*; a nuanced opinion, not a flat one

**Week 15 — Das Passiv & der Nominalstil: Amtsdeutsch überleben · M15** *(level B2)*
99. Vorgangspassiv — *werden* + Partizip II across tenses (*wird/wurde/ist… worden*); the process-passive of signs and news
100. Zustandspassiv vs Vorgangspassiv — *die Tür ist geschlossen* (state) vs *wird geschlossen* (event); the *sein*/*werden* split
101. The agent — *von* (the doer) vs *durch* (the means/cause); when to name an agent at all, and *man* as the active escape hatch
102. *sein + zu* + Infinitiv & alternatives — *das ist noch zu klären*; *lässt sich… machen*; passive-avoidance strategies
103. Nominalstil — turning verbs into nouns (*bearbeiten → die Bearbeitung*); decoding a Behörden-Brief; when to *un-nominalize* to sound human
