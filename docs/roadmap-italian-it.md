# ItalianBuddy — 84-Day Curriculum Roadmap (`italian-it`)

*Authoring-time design doc. Mirrors `plan.md` §3 in structure, but the sequence is
designed from **Italian's own grammar spine**, not translated from Dutch. This is the
locked scope-per-day map that lesson batches are written against.*

Pack id: **`italian-it-v1`** · App: **ItalianBuddy** · A1 → B1 · 84 days / 12 weeks.

---

## 1. Dialect identity

- **Identity:** *standard Italian* — the national, TV/broadcast standard (*italiano standard*,
  the register of the telegiornale, of national radio, of a schoolteacher). This is the
  neutral, everywhere-understood Italian.
- **Contrast:** **not a regional dialect.** Italy's dialetti (napoletano, siciliano,
  veneto, romanesco…) are real living languages, not "bad Italian" — but they are *not*
  what we teach. We stay in standard Italian while staying **aware of regional variety**:
  the learner is told, e.g., that a *cornetto* in the north is a *brioche* in parts of the
  south, that *anguria/cocomero/melone* splits by region, and that our Naples character
  Pietro speaks perfect standard Italian to the learner even though he'd switch to
  napoletano with his family. Regional colour is flagged, never taught as the target.
- **Markers to lean on** (national-standard spoken filler, safe everywhere):
  *allora, dai, boh, magari, insomma, va bene / vabbè, guarda, senti, cioè, comunque.*
  Never teach regional forms as standard (no *je/jamm* [nap.], *mona* [ven.], *'nvece* etc.).

## 2. Grammar spine (the 4 structural anchors that drive sequencing)

Italian's hard parts are different from Dutch's. Sequencing hangs on these four:

1. **The conjugation system** — three verb classes (**-are / -ere / -ire**, the last split
   into plain and **-isc-** types: *capire → capisco*), **subject-drop / pro-drop** (the
   ending carries the person, so *parlo* not *io parlo* by default), and the
   **high-frequency irregulars** you cannot avoid on day one (*essere, avere, fare, andare,
   stare, dare, venire, potere, volere, dovere*). Introduced W1–2, revisited forever.
2. **`passato prossimo` vs `imperfetto`** — the everyday-past split. Passato prossimo is
   built with **`essere` or `avere` + past participle**, with **participle agreement**
   (*sono andata*, *le ho viste*); imperfetto is the description/habit/background past.
   Choosing between them is *the* A2 storytelling skill. Passato prossimo W5, imperfetto
   W7, the contrast is the hinge of W7.
3. **Gender & number agreement, everywhere** — nouns are m/f, articles and adjectives must
   agree (*la casa bianca*, *i libri rossi*), and this reaches into participle agreement and
   the notorious **`piacere`** construction (*mi piace il caffè* / *mi piacciono i libri*).
   Seeded W1 (articles), compounded every week after.
4. **Clitic pronouns + the `congiuntivo`** as the B1 gate — direct/indirect object clitics
   and **`ci` / `ne`** (*lo vedo, gli parlo, ci penso, ne ho due*), their combinations
   (*glielo, me lo*), and the **`congiuntivo`** (*penso che sia…*). This cluster (W9–W10) is
   what separates a confident A2 from a real B1 speaker, and it's what learners most avoid.

## 5. Register / slang traffic-light

Taught explicitly on **day 79**, with cautions calibrated to Italian social taboos.

- 🟢 **Green — safe anywhere:** *dai, allora, magari, che bello!, volentieri, figurati,
  prego, boh, va bene / vabbè, ci sta, mitico* (mild). Warm, universal, un-embarrassing.
- 🟡 **Yellow — casual, read the room:** *che figata, che palle, un sacco (di), casino*
  ("a mess"), *cavolo!* (mild "darn"), *rompere le scatole, sto/stai messo male, sfigato.*
  Fine with friends, wrong with your landlord or in an office.
- 🔴 **Red — vulgar / taboo, flagged and explained, not for use:** *cazzo* and derivatives,
  *stronzo, vaffanculo, incazzarsi.* **Special Italian caution — le bestemmie (blasphemy):**
  religious blasphemy (*porco…* + a sacred name) is a category of its own in Italy — far more
  socially radioactive than ordinary vulgarity, taboo across generations and regions, and it
  is the one thing a foreigner should never repeat "to fit in." We name the category so the
  learner *recognises* it, and we hard-flag it: understand it, never say it. (This is Italian's
  analog to Dutch disease-based cursing — same "recognise, don't use" treatment.)

## 6. Meta-module slot — **day 74: "Il congiuntivo senza paura"**

Italian's language-specific meta-module. Where Dutch's day-74 tackles *"blijf in het
Nederlands"* (the switch-to-English reflex), Italian's tackles the single thing learners most
**avoid**: the **congiuntivo**. Learners dodge *penso che sia* by saying *penso che è* forever.
This module reframes it: which handful of triggers actually matter, the "if you skip it Italians
still understand you — but here's how to just do it" pep talk, three memorised scaffolds
(*penso che… / è meglio che… / spero che…*), and the confidence line **"quando sbagli il
congiuntivo, non succede niente."** It's a fear module, not a grammar-first module — matching
the Dutch slot's spirit (the thing you're scared of, and how to stop being scared).
*(Alternative considered: prosody + gesture, "parlare con tutto il corpo." Flagged in the
handoff report; congiuntivo chosen for the cleaner parallel to the Dutch avoidance-struggle.)*

  (*mi ha detto che…*). (reported-speech immersion)
- **day 69 (W10):** Sofia **takes the job and moves Bologna → Milano** — moving-day
  conversation braiding all of M10 (clitics + verb clusters). **THE mid-arc life event.**
- **~day 76 (W11):** Giulia **visits Sofia in Milano** — Saturday catch-up, Sofia settling in.
- **day 83 (W12):** everyone back in Bologna for an **aperitivo**, one year on.

**Continuity rule:** before day 62 Sofia lives in Bologna and knows nothing of Milano; 62–69
it's a decision in progress; from day 69 she lives in Milano. Never break this ordering.

## 8. Locale & audio config

- **`locale`: `it-IT`** — drives Web Speech API voice selection via the engine's `speak()`.
- Italian TTS is widely available (`it-IT` female/male voices ship on iOS/macOS/Android/Windows).
  No custom fallback needed for v1; the v2 pre-generated-TTS path is unaffected.
- Author `speak` = exact Italian to voice. Mind that TTS reads written stress poorly for
  minimal-pair drills (*àncora* vs *ancòra*, *pèsca* vs *pésca*) — for those, teach in the
  `card` and keep `speak` on the unambiguous member of the pair.

## 9. The 12-week curriculum (84 days)

Weekly rhythm (mirrors the engine's fixed rhythm): **W1–2** = 6 teaching days + capstone;
**W3–12** = 5 teaching + 1 review/immersion + 1 capstone. Every capstone ends with the growing
self-intro (*"il mio racconto"*, §10). Every review/immersion carries the cast arc.

### A1 Foundations — Weeks 1–4
*Greet, introduce yourself, order coffee, describe your day.*

**Week 1 (M01) — Suoni e prime parole** *(sounds & first words)*
1. Suoni I — the pure **7-vowel** system (a e i o u, open/closed *e* and *o*), **word stress**, no vowel-slurring
2. Suoni II — **c/g hard vs soft** (*ca/co/cu · che/chi* vs *ce/ci*; *ga/go/gu · ghe/ghi* vs *ge/gi*), **gn / gli / sc(i)**, **double consonants** (*pala* vs *palla*)
3. Saluti & il bar — *ciao/buongiorno/buonasera*, the coffee ritual, *per favore/grazie/prego*, ordering *un caffè*
4. Pronomi + **essere** — *io/tu/lui/lei/noi/voi/loro*; *sono/sei/è*; **tu vs Lei** intro; **subject-drop**
5. **avere** + negation — *ho/hai/ha*; *non* before the verb; *c'è / ci sono* first contact
6. Genere & articoli — *il/lo/la*, nouns in -o/-a/-e; plural -i/-e first contact; why gender drives everything later
7. **Capstone** — first real self-intro + journal *(layer 1)*

**Week 2 (M02) — Numeri, tempo, e i verbi** *(numbers, time, the verb engine)*
8. Numeri 0–100 — including the vowel-drop joins (*ventuno, trentotto*)
9. Prezzi, l'euro, big numbers — *quant'è?*, paying at the bar
10. L'ora, i giorni, i mesi — *che ore sono? le tre e mezza*, the *è l'una / sono le due* split
11. **Present tense of -are verbs** + subject-drop — the regular engine (*parlare, lavorare, abitare*)
12. **-ere & -ire verbs** (incl. **-isc-**: *capire → capisco*) + yes/no questions by intonation
13. **fare, andare, stare** — the irregulars you need immediately; *fare la spesa/colazione*, *andare a/in*
14. **Capstone** — daily-routine production day *(layer 2)*

**Week 3 (M03) — Persone e posti** *(people & places)*
15. La famiglia + **possessives** — *mio/mia/miei/mie*; the no-article-with-singular-family quirk (*mia madre*)
16. **Aggettivi & agreement** — endings agree in gender/number; position; *bello/buono*
17. **questo / quello** + showing photos — demonstrative agreement
18. Il tuo quartiere — neighborhood vocab, *la piazza*, *dov'è…?*, prepositions of place (*a/in/da*)
19. Descrivere le persone — looks & personality (*simpatico, gentile, in gamba*)
20. **Review + immersion** — meet **Giulia** narrating her Bologna quartiere
21. **Capstone** — family + quartiere portrait *(layer 3)*

**Week 4 (M04) — Cibo e i modali** *(food & the modal politeness system)*
22. Il cibo — meal structure (*colazione/pranzo/cena*), *primo/secondo*, Italian meal culture
23. Al bar e in trattoria — ordering: *vorrei, per me, mi porta…, il conto*
24. **Verbi modali** — *potere/volere/dovere* + infinitive (the politeness engine)
25. **mi piace / mi piacciono** — the *piacere* construction (it agrees with the *thing liked* — the classic trap)
26. Al mercato & al supermercato — quantities (*un etto, un chilo, una fetta*), paying
27. **Review + immersion** — **Pietro**'s routine and his favorite Naples pizzeria/bar
28. **Capstone** — full-A1 production day: order, describe, introduce *(layer 4)*

### A2 Building — Weeks 5–8
*Tell stories in the past. Travel, health, everyday life.*

**Week 5 (M05) — Il passato prossimo** *(Italian's everyday past)*
29. Passato prossimo with **avere** — regular participles -ato/-uto/-ito
30. Passato prossimo with **essere** — motion/change verbs + **participle agreement** (*sono andato/a*)
31. **Irregular participles** — the top 15 (*fatto, detto, preso, messo, visto, venuto, stato…*)
32. **essere vs avere** — reflexives take *essere*; verbs that take both; the choice rule
33. Viaggiare & i treni — Trenitalia, *regionale* vs *alta velocità*, *il binario*, *timbrare il biglietto*
34. **Review + immersion** — Giulia's weekend trip to the Cinque Terre (passato-prossimo narrative)
35. **Capstone** — *"cosa hai fatto?"* storytelling with Marco *(layer 5)*

**Week 6 (M06) — Salute, sentimenti, consigli** *(health, feelings, advice)*
36. Il corpo — body vocab, *mi fa male / mi fanno male i piedi* (agreement again)
37. Dal medico / in farmacia — the pharmacy-first culture, appointment dialogue
38. Emergenze — key phrases, *chiamare il 112*, *al pronto soccorso*
39. Le emozioni — *felice/arrabbiato/stanco/nervoso*; *sentirsi* + reflexive intro
40. Dare consigli — *devi…, dovresti…* (preview of conditional), *perché non…?*
41. **Review + immersion** — **Sofia's rough week** (scooter trouble, sick, farmacia)
42. **Capstone** — *"cosa è successo?"* production day *(layer 6)*

**Week 7 (M07) — L'altro passato: l'imperfetto & i ricordi** *(the other past & memories)*
43. L'imperfetto — endings -avo/-evo/-ivo; *ero/avevo/facevo*
44. **Passato prossimo vs imperfetto** — the usage split (event vs description/habit/background)
45. Da bambino/a — childhood vocab, school memories
46. Descrivere luoghi e tempo nel passato — weather & scene-setting in imperfetto
47. Prima vs adesso — habitual past, frequency adverbs (*sempre… mai*)
48. **Review + immersion** — Pietro's childhood in Naples (imperfetto)
49. **Capstone** — childhood portrait woven into the self-intro *(layer 7)*

**Week 8 (M08) — Futuro, opinioni, e la porta della subordinata** *(future, opinions, the gate)*
50. Il futuro — *futuro semplice* (*parlerò*) + *present-for-future*; time markers
51. Fare progetti — *pensare di + inf, avere intenzione di*; offers & predictions
52. Opinioni — *penso che, secondo me, credo che* (first taste of congiuntivo); the agreement ladder
53. **Subordinate clauses** — *perché, quando, se* + subordination (the A2→B1 gate)
54. Comparativi e superlativi — *più… di, il più…*, irregulars (*migliore, peggiore*)
55. **Review + immersion** — Giulia & Marco debate: must you learn Italian to live here?
56. **Capstone** — *"il mio prossimo anno"* production day *(layer 8)*

### B1 Threshold — Weeks 9–12
*Opinions, hypotheticals, real conversations — and stepping into the congiuntivo.*

**Week 9 (M09) — Frasi complesse & il congiuntivo** *(complex sentences & subjunctive)*
57. Il congiuntivo presente — forms; the trigger verbs (*penso che, voglio che, è importante che*)
58. Congiuntivo triggers — impersonal/emotion/doubt; and **when NOT** to use it (facts → indicativo)
59. Discorso indiretto — *mi ha detto che…*, reporting questions with *se*
60. Il condizionale — *vorrei, potrei, dovrei*; polite requests, hypotheticals
61. Il periodo ipotetico I — real conditions (*se… presente/futuro*)
62. **Review + immersion** — **Sofia's news: a job offer in Milano** (reported speech)
63. **Capstone** — *"la notizia"* gossip production day with Marco *(layer 9)*

**Week 10 (M10) — I pronomi & i verbi** *(clitic pronouns & verb clusters — the feared module)*
64. **Pronomi diretti** — *lo/la/li/le* + **past-participle agreement** (*l'ho vista*)
65. **Pronomi indiretti + ci & ne** — *gli/le, ci penso, ne ho due, ne parlo* — the two-day-worth module learners fear
66. **Pronomi combinati** — *glielo, me lo, ce n'è*; placement with infinitive/imperative
67. Verbi riflessivi & **si impersonale** — *mi alzo; si mangia bene*; in passato prossimo
68. **stare + gerundio & periphrases** — *sto facendo, sto per partire*; verb clusters
69. **Review + immersion** — **Sofia took the job: moving day**, braiding all of M10. *THE life event.*
70. **Capstone** — *"la mia grande decisione"* letter + portrait *(layer 10)*

**Week 11 (M11) — Parlare come un italiano** *(sounding Italian)*
71. Verbo + preposizione fissa — *pensare a, riuscire a, cominciare a, finire di* (core pairs)
72. **Le paroline & i segnali** — *allora, dai, magari, insomma, boh, va bene* — the soul of spoken Italian; blunt vs soft
73. **Gli alterati** — diminutives/augmentatives -ino/-etto/-one/-accio (*un caffettino, una parolaccia*); register shades
74. **Il congiuntivo senza paura** — the meta-module: why learners avoid it, three scaffolds, confidence scripts
75. **tu vs Lei** mastery — *dare del tu / dare del Lei*, politeness calibration by context
76. **Review + immersion** — Giulia visits Sofia in **Milano** (Saturday catch-up braiding M11)
77. **Capstone** — *"il mio racconto, formale e informale"* *(layer 11: register control)*

**Week 12 (M12) — Italiano di strada & il finale** *(street Italian & finale)*
78. L'imperativo — commands (*dai!, guarda!, senti!, vai*), tu/Lei/voi forms, softeners
79. **Slang traffic-light** — 🟢 / 🟡 / 🔴 (incl. le bestemmie taboo, flagged & explained — see §5)
80. Aperitivo & la vita sociale — ordering flow (*spritz, un tagliere, offro io*), rounds, *cin cin*
81. Dal barbiere / in negozio — service scenes, small-talk scripts
82. Modi di dire & proverbi — 12 idioms that don't translate (*in bocca al lupo, non tutte le ciambelle riescono col buco…*)
83. **Final immersion** — Giulia, Marco & Sofia at an aperitivo, one year on: every M12 move braided
84. **Final capstone — Il mio racconto** — 12-week recap, letter to your past self (one tense-move per slot), definitive B1 self-intro, Italian farewells, celebratory stats banner

## 10. The growing self-intro — "il mio racconto"

Every capstone ends with the learner extending a personal self-intro that gains exactly one
new grammar layer per week (cumulative — earlier layers still expected). Capstone authors MUST
append the correct week's layer via a `builder` or `journal` block titled around *"il mio racconto"*.

| Wk | Capstone day | New layer (cumulative) |
|----|------|------|
| 1  | 7  | name, where you're from, that you're learning Italian (present; *essere/avere*, *chiamarsi*) |
| 2  | 14 | + your daily routine (present tense, a reflexive *mi sveglio*, a time expression) |
| 3  | 21 | + your family & your quartiere (possessives, adjective agreement) |
| 4  | 28 | + what you like to eat/drink & a modal wish (*mi piace…, vorrei…, mi piacerebbe…*) |
| 5  | 35 | + something you **did** recently (passato prossimo) |
| 6  | 42 | + how you've been feeling / a small health event (*mi sentivo…, avevo…, mi faceva male…*) |
| 7  | 49 | + a childhood memory (imperfetto: *da bambino/a…*) |
| 8  | 56 | + a plan and an opinion (*andrò… / ho intenzione di…* + *penso che…*) |
| 9  | 63 | + something someone told you (reported speech: *… mi ha detto che…*) |
| 10 | 70 | + a big decision, with a clitic and a subclause (Sofia's move as the model: *ci ho pensato molto, perché…*) |
| 11 | 77 | + register control: the same intro said formally (*Lei*) vs casually (*tu*), plus one congiuntivo |
| 12 | 84 | **Il mio racconto, definitivo** — the whole thing, one tense-move per slot, a letter to your past self, Italian farewells |
